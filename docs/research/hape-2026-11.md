# Research swing: #70, #140, #249 (November 2026)

Exploratory measurements, not a calibration. Nothing here changes detector
behaviour, weights, labels, or catalog text.

- Detector revision: `933496df3bc0b06b3552f92a0be5f9f5e0f7f608` (main, 2026-11-03).
- Options: `contextMode: 'general'`, `sourceMode: 'plain'` (the `fp-measure.js` defaults).
- Scripts: [`hape-2026-11/`](hape-2026-11/). They print counts and summary
  statistics only. Corpus text stays in the gitignored `corpus/cache/`.

## Data

**Repository corpus** (`npm run corpus:fetch`). 25 of the 55 manifest sources
(all `cb-*` blog posts) returned HTTP 403 from this environment, so the human
side is 646 paragraph units, not the 875 in #70. Machine text is HC3
(ChatGPT, December 2022) and RAID (GPT-2 through GPT-4, Llama 2 chat, Mistral,
MPT, Cohere).

**HAP-E**, the [Human-AI Parallel English corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/browndw/human-ai-parallel-corpus)
(Reinhart et al., MIT licence). Each of 8,290 human texts in six registers
(academic, blog, fiction, news, spoken, TV/movie scripts) is split in two. The
models continue chunk 1, so human chunk 2 and each model's continuation are
paired on the same prompt. Used here: human chunk 2 (4.0M words), GPT-4o
2024-08-06 (4.4M), GPT-4o-mini 2024-07-18 (4.8M), Llama 3 70B Instruct (3.8M),
Llama 3 8B Instruct (3.5M).

HAP-E is not in `corpus/manifest.json`. Adding it is a provenance decision for
#72. Its limits for this repo:

- Continuation is not chat. Models imitating a supplied prompt should
  *understate* differences from free generation.
- Nothing from 2025 or later, and no Claude, Gemini, or GPT-5 output.
- No technical-blog, docs, LinkedIn, or marketing register, which is where
  several catalog tics are said to live.
- Full documents (median about 470 words), not 50–400-word paragraphs.

## #70: the 0–100 score

### Repository corpus (paragraph units)

| | human | machine |
|---|---|---|
| units | 646 | 775 |
| max score | 6 | 10 |
| score 0 | 87.8% | 75.5% |
| p95 / p99 | 2 / 5 | 5 / 10 |

ROC-AUC: score 0.567. Raw (undivided) score 0.566. Per-100-words 0.568. **Any
finding fired (yes/no): 0.561.** At paragraph length the whole 0–100 scale
ranks text about as well as one bit.

Document units give the same pattern: score AUC 0.693, any-hit 0.673, max 13.

Reweighting does not fix this. A five-fold logistic regression on per-category
counts reaches AUC 0.585 (paragraphs) and 0.681 (documents, *below* the
hand-set weights). Adding length as a feature lifts both above 0.73, but that
is a length artefact. In this corpus length alone separates the classes (AUC
0.31 paragraph, 0.66 document), and its direction flips between unit types. The
ceiling is set by recall: three quarters of machine paragraphs trigger
nothing.

### HAP-E (documents)

| | human | GPT-4o | GPT-4o-mini | Llama 3 70B-I | Llama 3 8B-I |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| max score | 9 | 34 | 28 | 21 | 17 |
| score 0 | 72% | 10.5% | 11.7% | 41% | 44% |
| p50 / p90 / p99 | 0 / 2 / 3 | 4 / 12 / 19 | 4 / 14 / 20 | 1 / 5 / 9 | 1 / 4 / 8 |
| score > 15 ("Some AI patterns" or worse) | 0% | 3.7% | 6.3% | 0.04% | 0.01% |
| ROC-AUC vs human | | **0.905** | 0.896 | 0.695 | 0.675 |
| AUC, any-hit bit | | 0.808 | 0.802 | 0.655 | 0.640 |
| flag at ≥ 3 (human p95): FPR / TPR | | 2.5% / **70.4%** | 2.5% / 69.6% | 2.5% / 28.3% | 2.5% / 24.0% |
| paired: model scores higher than its own human twin | | 87.3% | 85.7% | 52.1% | 48.4% |

Per-register AUC for GPT-4o: academic 0.998, news 0.960, spoken 0.939, blog
0.891, fiction 0.871, TV/movie 0.848.

What this changes:

1. The engine does separate 2024 GPT-4-family text: AUC 0.9, and 70% caught at
   a 2.5% human false-positive rate. The near-coin-flip in `PROOF.md` and #70 is
   mostly a property of the 2022-era machine corpus, not of the engine.
2. The scale is still unused. GPT-4o's p99 is 19, and **96% of GPT-4o
   documents get "Clean" or "Minimal AI signals"** even though the score ranks
   them well. #70's label complaint holds and is now sharper: the number is
   informative and the label hides it.
3. Options 1 and 3 in #70 (rescale or move band edges) are monotone, so they
   cannot change ranking. They only change where the labels fall. Option 2
   (human-percentile bands) makes the number checkable: "≥ 3 is above 95% of
   human documents" holds on both corpora (repository document p95 = 3, HAP-E
   p95 = 2). Bands need a register and a unit, because HAP-E human text sits
   lower than the repository's.
4. Model family matters as much as the threshold. Llama 3 Instruct output
   scores close to human. A single "AI-likelihood" label will be wrong for one
   family or the other.

Suggested next step for #70 (still a decision, not a change): publish
human-percentile bands per unit (paragraph, document) from the provenance
corpus, labelled with the corpus revision, and keep the raw score visible.
Re-run against #72's modern-machine corpus before choosing band labels.

## #140: recall cost of the rhetorical-tic carve-outs

Each boundary was loosened one at a time with standalone regexes copied from
`detector/patterns.js`. Counts are raw text matches; "detector" means the
shipping engine reported the same span.

### Repository corpus (235k words)

Every carve-out form has zero to one occurrence in either class: one 2-item
"No X, no Y" chain (RAID machine text) and one "it just works" (HC3 human,
inside single quotes). **The corpus cannot measure these trade-offs.** With
zero hits in 115k machine words, the 95% upper bound is about 26 per million
words, so it cannot rule out a common tic either.

### HAP-E (4.0M human, 16.5M machine words)

| form | human | GPT-4o | 4o-mini | L3-70B-I | L3-8B-I |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| current: sentence-initial "No …" chain, 3+ items | 2 | 0 | 2 | 13 | 10 |
| loosen to 2 items (all) | 14 | 28 | 22 | 28 | 18 |
| …new hits beyond current | 12 | 28 | 20 | 15 | 8 |
| 2 items **and** a following "just / only / simply" | **1** | **12** | **10** | **6** | **4** |
| lowercase "no …" after colon or dash, 2+ | 4 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 2 |
| unanchored "no …" chain, 3+ (control) | 11 | 3 | 3 | 41 | 35 |
| "the punchline" (any use) | 1 | 7 | 10 | 1 | 3 |
| rhetorical "here's the punchline" / "the punchline is:" | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| "worth naming" | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| "batteries included" | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| "it just works" with Unicode-dash "out‑of‑the‑box" | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Readings (hits inspected locally):

- **Two-item chains, ungated: keep out.** Human hits are mostly screenplay
  dialogue and terse diary blogging ("No priors, no convictions."). Loosening
  adds about 3 human false positives per million words to gain about 4.3
  machine hits per million.
- **Two-item chains followed by "just …": candidate for a context-gated
  branch.** 32 new machine hits in 16.5M words against one human hit in 4.0M
  (about 1.9 vs 0.25 per million, roughly 8×). The "No X, no Y. Just Z." cadence
  is the shape that shows up in every model's output. Counts are small, so this
  needs must-fire and must-not-fire fixtures and a second corpus before
  promotion.
- **Lowercase after a colon or dash: keep out.** It is human-leaning here (4 vs
  0–2).
- **Sentence anchor: confirmed as worth it.** Dropping it multiplies human hits
  by 5.5 (2 → 11) while Llama gains about 3×.
- **"the punchline": stay judgment-only.** It leans machine (21 of 22 hits) but
  every hit was a literal joke reference in fiction, scripts, or chatty blog
  text. The rhetorical tic the catalog names never occurred.
- **"worth naming", "batteries included", Unicode-dash form:** zero everywhere.
  These look like 2025-era assistant or developer-marketing tics. No available
  corpus can measure them; #72 is the dependency.
- **Quote masking:** 0–5 hits per form fell inside an open double quote. It is
  not the binding constraint.

Byproducts (not fixed here):

- `ITEM` allows `-` inside a word, so an ASCII hyphen used as a dash welds the
  next word into the span: "no future-just the".
- The second-word stop list lacks `and`, `or`, and `but`, so "no foreigners
  and" is captured. This adds to #140's known lexical-verb capture ("no parents
  breathing").
- Single-quoted phrases are not masked, by design (apostrophes). HC3's
  `' it just works '` is the one human dev-blog hit in the repository corpus.

## #249: uncited "AI writes X" claims

The approved batch is @shudhanshu002's to choose, so no section text is edited
here. This adds a third option beside cite and soften: **measure**. Eight
claims are testable on HAP-E. Regexes are deliberately rough, so read rates as
direction and order of magnitude.

| section | claim | human | GPT-4o | 4o-mini | L3-70B-I | L3-8B-I | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rhythm and uniformity | "AI text is metronomic; human text has varied rhythm" | sentence-length CV median 0.65 | 0.40 | 0.44 | 0.43 | 0.41 | **Supported.** AUC 0.84–0.89; holds in all six registers for all four models |
| Vocabulary diversity | "AI text trends flatter, sometimes drifting under 0.40" (TTR, 200 words) | median 0.63; 64% in 0.50–0.65 | 0.70 | 0.70 | 0.61 | 0.59 | **Family-dependent.** GPT-4o is *more* diverse than humans; Llama is slightly less. Under 0.40: 0–1% of any class |
| Colon into a triple | "The most common shape LLM prose uses" | 12 / M words | 26 | 27 | 26 | 19 | **Direction supported (about 2×), "most common" not.** About 25 per million words |
| Same-opener sentence runs | "LLMs reach for it constantly" | 499 / M (3+ runs) | 114 | 133 | 831 | 1073 | **Family-dependent.** GPT-4o about 4× *below* human; Llama about 2× above |
| Stranded auxiliary contrast | "a signature LLM move" | 9.1 / M | 0.7 | 0.6 | 1.1 | 0.3 | **Reversed in 2024 models.** May describe newer models; unmeasured |
| Copula avoidance | "serves as / boasts / stands as" | 36 / M | 277 | 307 | 65 | 51 | **Supported**, 1.4–8.5× (already softened in #329; this could now be cited) |
| Hyphenated modifier stacking | "AI stacks compound modifiers" | 53 / M | 43 | 32 | 43 | 43 | **Not supported** in this corpus |
| Confidence calibration | "Notably, / Interestingly, …" density | 23 / M | 54 | 62 | 4.7 | 0.6 | **Family-dependent.** GPT about 2.5× above; Llama 5–40× below |

The main result is that "AI" is not one writer. Three of eight claims hold for
one model family and reverse for another, and the detector's `low-ttr` and
`uniformity` categories inherit that split. A claim that survives should name
the family and date it was measured on.

## Reproduce

```bash
npm run corpus:fetch
bash docs/research/hape-2026-11/fetch-hape.sh        # needs pyarrow
node docs/research/hape-2026-11/score70.js paragraph  # #70, repository corpus
node docs/research/hape-2026-11/score70.js document
node docs/research/hape-2026-11/carve140.js           # #140, repository corpus
node docs/research/hape-2026-11/hape.js               # #70 + #140 on HAP-E (about 3 min)
node docs/research/hape-2026-11/gate140.js            # #140 two-item gate
node docs/research/hape-2026-11/claims249.js          # #249 claims
```

Add `--show` to `carve140.js` or `hape.js` to print matched excerpts to
stderr for local inspection. Do not paste corpus excerpts into issues beyond
short quotations.
